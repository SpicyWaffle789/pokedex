About Pokémon

The Beginning

Pokémon was created by Satoshi Tajiri in 1989. As a child, Tajiri loved collecting insects — a popular hobby in rural Japan. That passion for collecting, discovering, and cataloging creatures became the foundation of Pokémon.

The name "Pokémon" is a shortening of the Japanese title Poketto Monsutā (Pocket Monsters). Tajiri envisioned a world where children could catch, train, and trade creatures, mirroring the joy he felt exploring fields and streams as a kid.

Game Freak & Nintendo

Tajiri co-founded Game Freak in 1989 with fellow game enthusiasts. After several small projects, he pitched his creature-collecting idea to Nintendo. Initially skeptical, Nintendo eventually greenlit the project after seeing early prototypes.

Development of the first games took nearly six years. Tajiri's perfectionism and ambitious vision pushed the team to their limits. During development, many believed the project would fail. The breakthrough came when Shigeru Miyamoto (creator of Mario and Zelda) became a mentor figure, helping guide the project to completion.

Pokémon Red & Green (1996)

The first Pokémon games — Pokémon Red and Green — launched in Japan on February 27, 1996, for the Game Boy. Sales were initially slow, but word of mouth spread quickly. Within months, the games became a phenomenon.

The core formula was deceptively simple: catch 'em all. Players could find 151 unique creatures across the Kanto region, each with unique types, stats, and evolutions. The Game Boy's link cable allowed players to trade Pokémon and battle friends — creating a social experience unlike anything before.

The Trading Card Game

In October 1996, the Pokémon Trading Card Game (TCG) launched in Japan. Created by Creatures Inc. (led by Tajiri's friend Tsukasa Good-Nature), the cards featured original artwork and strategic gameplay.

The TCG arrived in North America in 1999 and became a worldwide collecting craze. Rare holographic cards like First Edition Charizard became legendary — some now sell for hundreds of thousands of dollars. The TCG remains one of the most popular card games in the world, with over 40 billion cards sold globally.

The Anime

The Pokémon anime debuted in Japan on April 1, 1997. Ash Ketchum (Satoshi in Japanese) and his loyal Pikachu set out to become Pokémon Masters. The show introduced millions of viewers to the world of Pokémon and became one of the longest-running anime series in history.

The anime's influence cannot be overstated — it turned Pikachu into a global icon and kept interest in the franchise burning bright between game releases.

Generations & Evolution

Each new generation of games introduces new Pokémon, regions, and mechanics:

  • Gen I (1996) — Kanto: 151 Pokémon. Red, Blue, Green, Yellow.
  • Gen II (1999) — Johto: 100 new. Gold, Silver, Crystal. Introduced breeding, day/night cycles.
  • Gen III (2002) — Hoenn: 135 new. Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald. Abilities, natures, double battles.
  • Gen IV (2006) — Sinnoh: 107 new. Diamond, Pearl, Platinum. Physical/special split.
  • Gen V (2010) — Unova: 156 new. Black, White. Seasonal changes, seasons.
  • Gen VI (2013) — Kalos: 72 new. X, Y. Mega Evolution, 3D graphics.
  • Gen VII (2016) — Alola: 81 new. Sun, Moon. Z-Moves, regional forms.
  • Gen VIII (2019) — Galar: 89 new. Sword, Shield. Dynamax, open-world Wild Area.
  • Gen IX (2022) — Paldea: 120+ new. Scarlet, Violet. Open world, Terastallization.

Cultural Impact

Pokémon is the highest-grossing media franchise of all time, generating over $100 billion in revenue. It spans video games, movies, anime, cards, merchandise, theme parks, and mobile games like Pokémon GO (2016), which became a global cultural phenomenon.

The franchise has sold over 480 million video games worldwide. Pikachu appears on everything from planes to postage stamps. In 2016, Pokémon GO got millions of people outside walking, exploring, and socializing — fulfilling Satoshi Tajiri's original dream of connecting people through the joy of discovery.

About This App

This Pokédex is a fan-made reference tool built with data from the PokéAPI. It features:

  • Complete Pokédex with 1025+ Pokémon, stats, types, and evolutions
  • TCG card sets, individual cards, and marketplace pricing links
  • Browseable attacks and abilities database
  • Pokémon Wordle and mini-games
  • Pokémon Champions battle mode with evolution, leveling, and catching
  • Number Roll (RNGdle) badge collection game
  • Card collection tracker
  • Dark/light theme toggle
